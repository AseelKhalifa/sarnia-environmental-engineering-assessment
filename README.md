# Sarnia-environmental-engineering-assessment
A civil and environmental engineering research project assessing the sustainability of Sarnia’s Chemical Valley, evaluating industrial pollution, infrastructure performance, environmental justice, and proposed engineering mitigation strategies.
Sarnia’s Chemical Valley — Environmental Engineering & Sustainability Assessment

Project Overview:
- This project investigates the environmental, economic, and social impacts of Sarnia’s Chemical Valley, one of Canada’s major petrochemical industrial hubs.
- Developed from a collaborative academic research project at McMaster University, this portfolio includes a sustainability assessment poster and a supplementary research paper exploring civil and environmental engineering solutions for sustainable industrial development.

Research Objectives:
- Assess industrial impacts on air, water, soil, and groundwater quality.
- Evaluate industrial infrastructure through the three pillars of sustainability.
- Examine environmental justice and the impacts on Aamjiwnaang First Nation.
- Identify opportunities for sustainable infrastructure modernization.
- Propose engineering strategies for pollution mitigation and environmental management.

Engineering Applications:
- Environmental Impact Assessment
- Sustainable Stormwater Management
- Industrial Wastewater Treatment
- Soil and Groundwater Remediation
- GIS-Based Environmental Risk Mapping
- Infrastructure Asset Management
- Sustainable Land-Use Planning
- Life-Cycle Cost Assessment

Project Deliverables:
- Sustainability Assessment Poster: Collaborative research evaluating the environmental, economic, and social performance of Sarnia’s Chemical Valley.
- Supplementary Research Paper: An extended investigation proposing conceptual engineering solutions, sustainability strategies, and a phased implementation framework.

Research Methodology:
The project applies qualitative case-study analysis, secondary environmental data, government publications, and sustainability performance indicators to evaluate industrial development and its associated challenges.

Key Takeaway:
Sustainable industrial development requires an integrated engineering approach that balances infrastructure performance, economic resilience, environmental protection, and community well-being.
